This is a running order, not a ranking

Songs are chosen by score, then ordered for how the night should feel — strong open, dip a third in, heavy at the peak, slow moment after it, big finish. So the highest-scoring song is usually not first: a natural closer is deliberately placed last. For pure score order, use the Full ranking tab.

How this order was built

  1. Locked organiser requests go in automatically. For Creed and Nickelback, whichever song has the most X marks wins.
  2. Everything else is pure total score — highest first, taken from the top until the set hits ~90 minutes (25s of changeover assumed per song). Nothing is promoted or demoted against the vote.
  3. No more than two songs by any one band — counted across the locked requests too. Because the list is score-ordered, a band keeps its two best and the rest drop, marked CAP on the ranking tab.
  4. No two slow songs sit next to each other, and no four-song stretch is allowed to sag — a trough is what empties a floor, and it is usually built from mid-tempo songs that each look fine on their own.
  5. Where the result looks risky — no slow song, too little for the female vocalist, a long unbroken run for V1 — it says so in red, but it does not overrule you.
  6. The order is then built against an energy curve: strong open, a dip around a third in, the heavy cluster at ~60–70%, the dedication ballad straight after the peak, then a lift into the closer.
  7. No three songs in a row share a lead vocalist, and no two songs by the same artist sit next to each other.
